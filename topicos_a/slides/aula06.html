Mastering the Descent: From SGD to Adam

1. Pathological Curvature

Why does Vanilla SGD fail?

The "Ravine"

Imagine a valley that is:

  • Very Steep in the vertical direction ($y$).
  • Very Flat in the horizontal direction ($x$).

Gradient Descent points mostly in the steep direction, causing it to overshoot and bounce back and forth, making very little progress along the actual path.

2. Momentum

Accumulating velocity to damp oscillations.

$$ v_{t+1} = \rho v_t + \nabla L(w_t) $$ $$ w_{t+1} = w_t - \eta v_{t+1} $$

Parameters

Param Typical Effect
$\eta$ (Learning Rate) 0.01 Step size.
$\rho$ (Momentum) 0.9 Friction/Mass. Determines how fast the velocity decays. High $\rho$ means heavier ball (smoother).

3. RMSProp

Root Mean Square Propagation. Adapted for non-stationary settings.

The Math

1. Accumulate Squared Gradients:

$$ s_t = \beta s_{t-1} + (1-\beta) (\nabla L)^2 $$

2. Update Weights:

$$ w_{t+1} = w_t - \frac{\eta}{\sqrt{s_t + \epsilon}} \nabla L $$

Intuition

We normalize the gradient by its recent magnitude.

  • Steep direction: Large $(\nabla L)^2 \to$ Large $s_t \to$ Small update. (Damps oscillation)
  • Flat direction: Small $s_t \to$ Large update. (Speeds up)

4. Adam (Adaptive Moment Estimation)

Combines Momentum (First Moment) and RMSProp (Second Moment).

$$ m_t = \beta_1 m_{t-1} + (1-\beta_1) \nabla L \quad \text{(Momentum)} $$ $$ v_t = \beta_2 v_{t-1} + (1-\beta_2) (\nabla L)^2 \quad \text{(RMSProp)} $$ $$ \hat{m}_t = \frac{m_t}{1-\beta_1^t}, \quad \hat{v}_t = \frac{v_t}{1-\beta_2^t} \quad \text{(Bias Corr.)} $$ $$ w_{t+1} = w_t - \frac{\eta}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_t} + \epsilon} \hat{m}_t $$

Adam Hyperparameters

Param Default Effect
$\eta$ (Alpha) 0.001 Global learning rate.
$\beta_1$ 0.9 Decay for Momentum. Controls smoothness of direction.
$\beta_2$ 0.999 Decay for Squared Gradients. Controls the window for variance estimation. High = long memory.
$\epsilon$ (Epsilon) 1e-8 Stability. Prevents division by zero.

Summary

  • SGD oscillates in ravines.
  • Momentum fixes this by accumulating velocity ($\rho$).
  • RMSProp fixes this by scaling updates by standard deviation ($\sqrt{s_t}$).
  • Adam does BOTH. It is the gold standard.
  • Most frameworks initialize Adam with $\beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.999$.
