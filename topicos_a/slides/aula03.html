Thiago Medeiros

Measuring Success and Guiding Optimization

1. The Optimization Landscape

Training a Neural Network implies traversing a high-dimensional landscape to find the lowest valley (minimum loss).

$$ \theta^* = \arg \min_{\theta} J(\theta) $$
  • $\theta$: The model parameters (weights $W$, biases $b$).
  • $J(\theta)$: The Loss Function (or Cost Function).
  • Goal: Find $\theta$ such that $J$ is close to 0.

Loss vs Metric

It is crucial to distinguish these two concepts.

Properties Loss Function ($J$) Evaluation Metric
Purpose Guiding the optimizer (Gradient Descent). Human interpretation & Business rules.
Requirement Must be Differentiable (smooth). Can be discrete (e.g., Accuracy, F1).
Examples Cross-Entropy, MSE, Hinge Loss. Accuracy, Precision, AUC-ROC.

2. Regression Loss: Mean Squared Error

Used when predicting continuous values (e.g., House Prices).

$$ MSE = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2 $$

Properties

  • Quadratic Penalty: Large errors are punished severely.
  • Convexity: In linear regression, it implies a single global minimum.
  • Probabilistic View: Equivalent to Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) assuming Gaussian Noise.

Code


import torch.nn as nn
criterion = nn.MSELoss()

loss = criterion(prediction, target)
loss.backward()

Classification Loss: Cross-Entropy

Used for categorization. It measures the "distance" between two probability distributions.

$$ CE = - \sum_{i} y_i \log(\hat{y}_i) $$

Information Theory connection:

It relates to Kullback-Leibler (KL) Divergence. Minimizing CE is minimizing the surprise of seeing the true label given our prediction.

Why not MSE for Classification?

  • MSE assumes data comes from a Normal distribution (Class labels don't).
  • MSE with Sigmoid leads to non-convex landscapes (many local minima).
  • Cross-Entropy leads to steeper gradients when wrong, speeding up learning.

3. The Accuracy Trap

Accuracy is the fraction of correct predictions. It can be misleading on imbalanced datasets.

Scenario: Cancer Detection

  • Healthy: 990 patients
  • Cancer: 10 patients

Dummy Model: Predicts "Healthy" for everyone.

  • Accuracy: 99% (Wow!)
  • Utility: 0% (All cancer cases missed)

This is why we need richer metrics based on the Confusion Matrix.

Predicted Positive Predicted Negative
Actual Positive True Positive (TP) False Negative (FN)
Actual Negative False Positive (FP) True Negative (TN)

Precision & Recall

Precision

"Of all predicted positives, how many are actually positive?"

$$ P = \frac{TP}{TP + FP} $$

Important when False Positives are expensive (e.g., Spade Filter blocking real emails).

Recall (Sensitivity)

"Of all actual positives, how many did we find?"

$$ R = \frac{TP}{TP + FN} $$

Important when False Negatives are dangerous (e.g., Cancer Detection).

F1-Score

The Harmonic Mean of Precision and Recall. Punishment for extreme values.

$$ F1 = 2 \cdot \frac{P \cdot R}{P + R} $$
