Transfer Learning & Fine Tuning

Standing on the Shoulders of Giants (like ResNet)

Thiago Medeiros

Universidade do Estado do Rio de Janeiro

1. The Data Problem

Modern Deep Learning architectures have incredible capacity, but this comes at a steep price.

The Reality of Deep Learning

  • Deep Networks have tens or hundreds of millions of parameters.
  • To train them from scratch (random weights), you need millions of labeled images.
  • Training a ResNet-50 from scratch on a dataset of 1,000 images will immediately result in catastrophic Overfitting.
The Question

"I only have 1,000 labeled images of rare medical X-rays. Can I still use Deep Learning to classify them?"

YES!

Via Transfer Learning.

2. The ImageNet Foundation

ImageNet is the "Encyclopedia" of Computer Vision. It is the dataset upon which almost all modern CNNs are originally trained.

  • 1.2 Million high-resolution training images.
  • 1,000 diverse object classes (dogs, cats, cars, plants...).
  • State-of-the-art models (ResNet, VGG, EfficientNet) have been trained on this data for weeks on clusters of GPUs to reach peak performance.

The Core Idea: These models have already learned the fundamental physics of how to "see" the world. We can reuse that generic knowledge.

The ImageNet Challenge (Error Rate)

Why Transfer Learning Works

CNNs learn a highly structured hierarchy of features. The early layers are entirely generic, while only the final layers are task-specific.

3. Strategy 1: Feature Extraction

The Logic

  1. Take a pre-trained network (e.g., ResNet18).
  2. Freeze the entire Convolutional Base (requires_grad = False).
  3. Replace the final Fully Connected Layer (Classifier) to match your number of classes.
  4. Train only the new, randomly initialized classifier.

When to use?

  • You have a very Small Dataset.
  • Your images are visually similar to ImageNet (e.g., everyday objects, animals, vehicles).

Since the base is frozen, training is extremely fast and requires very little GPU memory!

Code: Feature Extraction

import torch
from torchvision import models
import torch.nn as nn

# 1. Carregar Modelo Pré-treinado no ImageNet
model = models.resnet18(weights=models.ResNet18_Weights.DEFAULT)

# 2. Congelar a Base Convolucional
for param in model.parameters():
    param.requires_grad = False

# 3. Substituir a 'Cabeça' de Classificação
# A última camada do ResNet chama-se 'fc' (Fully Connected)
num_features = model.fc.in_features

# Por defeito, uma nova camada TEM requires_grad=True
# Ex: Queremos classificar apenas entre Cão (0) e Gato (1)
model.fc = nn.Linear(num_features, 2) 

# Passar para o optimizador APENAS os parâmetros da nova camada!
optimizer = torch.optim.Adam(model.fc.parameters(), lr=1e-3)

4. Strategy 2: Fine-Tuning

The Logic

  1. Initialize with pre-trained weights.
  2. Replace the final classifier.
  3. Unfreeze all (or the top few) convolutional layers.
  4. Train the entire network, but use a very small learning rate.

When to use?

  • You have a Large Dataset.
  • Your domain is very different from ImageNet (e.g., Medical X-Rays, Satellite Imagery).

Caution: If you use a high learning rate during Fine-Tuning, the massive gradients coming from the randomly initialized classifier will propagate backwards and "destroy" the delicate pre-trained weights in the convolutional base!

Code: Fine-Tuning (Differential LRs)

The professional way to Fine-Tune is to assign a tiny learning rate to the pre-trained base, and a normal learning rate to the new classifier.

# 1. Carregar modelo e substituir a cabeça
model = models.resnet18(weights=models.ResNet18_Weights.DEFAULT)
model.fc = nn.Linear(model.fc.in_features, 2)

# 2. Separar os parâmetros em "Grupos"
base_params = [p for name, p in model.named_parameters() if 'fc' not in name]
head_params = model.fc.parameters()

# 3. Optimizador com Differential Learning Rates!
optimizer = torch.optim.Adam([
    # Base: Taxa de aprendizagem minúscula para não destruir os pesos
    {'params': base_params, 'lr': 1e-5}, 
    
    # Cabeça: Taxa de aprendizagem normal para treinar do zero
    {'params': head_params, 'lr': 1e-3}  
])

5. Hall of Fame: VGG (2014)

Philosophy: "Keep it Simple"

  • Uses only $3 \times 3$ convolutions and $2 \times 2$ max pooling.
  • Increases depth simply by stacking more identical layers (VGG-16 or VGG-19).
  • Pros: Extremely easy to understand and implement. Excellent feature extractor.
  • Cons: Massively heavy (138 Million parameters!). Slow to train and infer.

Hall of Fame: Inception/GoogLeNet (2014)

Instead of stacking layers sequentially, why not apply multiple kernel sizes ($1 \times 1$, $3 \times 3$, $5 \times 5$) in parallel and concatenate them?

The $1 \times 1$ Convolution Trick

A $1 \times 1$ convolution looks at a single spatial pixel but traverses the entire depth (channels).

It is used as a Bottleneck to drastically reduce the number of channels before an expensive $3 \times 3$ or $5 \times 5$ convolution, saving massive amounts of compute.

Hall of Fame: ResNet (2015)

Philosophy: "Let it Flow"

  • Problem: In very deep networks (>30 layers), gradients vanish during backprop. Performance degrades.
  • Solution: Residual Connections (Skip Connections).
  • Allows stable training of 100+ layers (ResNet-50, ResNet-101). Still the default baseline today.
The Residual Block
Gradient Flow: By definition, the derivative of $x$ is $1$. During backprop: $\frac{\partial}{\partial x} (F(x) + x) = \frac{\partial F(x)}{\partial x} + \mathbf{1}$. Even if $\frac{\partial F}{\partial x}$ vanishes to $0$, the gradient flows perfectly via the $+1$ path!

MobileNet & EfficientNet

Modern architectures shifted focus from purely "deeper" to Computational Efficiency.

MobileNet

  • Uses "Depthwise Separable Convolutions".
  • Splits the $3 \times 3 \times C$ operation into two steps: one spatial ($3 \times 3$ per channel) and one depthwise ($1 \times 1$ across channels).
  • Drastically reduces parameters. Great for edge devices/phones.

EfficientNet

  • Uses Compound Scaling.
  • Instead of just making the network deeper (ResNet) or wider (Inception), it optimizes Depth, Width, and Resolution simultaneously via a mathematical formula.
  • Yields state-of-the-art accuracy with 10x fewer parameters.

6. Strategy Decision Matrix

Dataset Size Similarity to ImageNet Recommended Strategy
Small High (Everyday Objects) Feature Extraction. Freeze the entire base, train only the head. Avoids overfitting.
Large High Fine Tuning. Unfreeze all layers, use differential learning rates.
Small Low (Medical, Satellite) Trouble. Freeze early layers (edges), fine-tune deeper layers carefully.
Large Low Train from Scratch (or heavy Fine-Tuning).

Summary & Next Steps

  • Transfer Learning: Reusing knowledge (weights) from networks trained on massive datasets (ImageNet) to solve tasks with limited data.
  • Feature Extraction: Freezing the base and treating it as a generic pattern detector.
  • Fine-Tuning: Slowly adjusting the entire network using very small learning rates.
  • Architectures:
    • VGG: Simple but heavy.
    • Inception: $1 \times 1$ convolutions for efficiency.
    • ResNet: Skip connections to prevent vanishing gradients.
